What are Zero-Width Characters and Blind Watermarks?

Unicode contains several non-printing control codepoints that take up zero pixels on a display screen. Common examples include U+200B (Zero-Width Space), U+200C (Zero-Width Non-Joiner), U+200D (Zero-Width Joiner), and U+FEFF (Zero-Width No-Break Space / BOM).

While originally designed for non-Latin script typography, these invisible codepoints are increasingly weaponized for steganographic blind watermarking: internal forums or corporate document portals secretly encode user IDs or employee badge numbers into binary strings of zero-width spaces when text is copied. If a document leaks, the company extracts the hidden characters to unmask the leaker.

Why Cleanse Your Code & Data?

  • Prevent Syntax Failures: Invisible characters accidentally pasted into code (Python, JavaScript, JSON, YAML) cause inexplicable SyntaxError or indent mismatches that standard IDEs fail to highlight.
  • Sanitize Copied Articles: Strip stealth tracking fingerprints before publishing research or migrating public documents.
  • SEO & URL Slugs: Prevent hidden Unicode from breaking clean search engine URLs or database indexing.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q: Will stripping invisible characters damage normal spaces and punctuation?

No. The sanitization algorithm targets specific non-printable codepoints (U+200B–U+200F, U+FEFF, U+00AD, etc.). Standard ASCII spaces (U+0020), tabs, carriage returns, and newlines are completely preserved.

Q: Is my text uploaded or scanned on a remote server?

Never. ToolNexus performs all character inspections and sanitization inside your browser memory. Your sensitive text and clipboard content never leave your machine.
