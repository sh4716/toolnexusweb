Why WCAG Contrast Compliance Matters

Over 2.2 billion people worldwide live with vision impairment or color blindness (protanopia, deuteranopia, tritanopia). The Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) published by the World Wide Web Consortium (W3C) define technical benchmarks to ensure digital interfaces remain perceptible to all human eyes.

In many jurisdictions (including the European Accessibility Act and the US Americans with Disabilities Act / Section 508), maintaining WCAG 2.1 Level AA compliance is not merely good design hygiene—it is a legal mandate. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q: What defines 'Large Text' in WCAG criteria?

Large text is defined as at least 18 point (24 CSS pixels) or 14 point bold (typically 18.66 CSS pixels with bold weight 700+).

Q: How is relative luminance calculated?

Relative luminance represents the relative brightness of any point in a colorspace, normalized to 0 for black and 1 for white. The contrast ratio formula is (L1 + 0.05) / (L2 + 0.05), where L1 is the lighter color and L2 is the darker color.
